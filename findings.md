- Top-level container full-match solves same-sentence scope but not cross-sentence attribution by itself. SHA `863B117E...` is bypassed by discourse adverbs (`On reflection`, `On second thought`, `To be clear`), filler sentences before a denial, and paraphrased meta/reporting clauses. The safer structural rule is to reject an assert when any later top-level negation/retraction cannot be proven to have a different explicit subject, and to block candidates preceded anywhere by token-class meta/directive/nonfactual/reporting markers.
- Forward-meta governance is bidirectional state, not just a marker to skip. SHA `23DDB9CA...` skips `Ignore the next sentence` itself but lets the governed negative target retract an earlier true witness. Precompute governed sentence indexes/spans so marker and target are both unable to produce edges or to affect prior asserted evidence.
- “Explicitly different subject” must not be inferred from a partial name. SHA `1E4B4F17...` treats later `Alice` or `Smith` as different from earlier `Alice Smith`. For fail-closed identity, any normalized name-token overlap—or canonical substring overlap for non-space scripts—blocks the different-subject exemption; only disjoint explicit names may preserve the earlier edge.
- Identity overlap also includes conventional compact/acronym forms. SHA `B2050036...` misses `Alice Smith → AS`, `Mary Jane Watson → MJW`, and `International Business Machines → IBM`. Two-or-more single-character name tokens (including normalized punctuation-separated initials) must collapse to the same abbreviation representation before the bidirectional ordered-initial check.
- Sentence-initial article protection must yield to a structurally proven initials sequence. SHA `04A61081...` rejects `A-X`/`A X`/`A.X` before identity comparison because of its generic `A` article guard, creating direction-dependent withdrawal behavior. Only two-or-more separated uppercase single-letter initials may bypass the article guard; ordinary `A company/person` must remain excluded.
- Forward governance requires clause-level spans inside the marker sentence. SHA `CF7BE348...` excludes the whole sentence when it contains `ignore/the next sentence`, thereby swallowing an independent `I do not...` or `I retract...` in the same sentence. Only the marker clause is non-evidentiary; marker-external withdrawal material must remain visible, while the next governed target sentence remains fully excluded.
- Clause spans cannot be inferred from a finite separator list. SHA `EC37A5B3...` masks an entire em-dash/colon/`yet`/`while` segment when it contains a forward marker, swallowing nearby withdrawal. The safe unit is the minimal absolute token span covering the marker scaffold; every token outside those spans remains subject to normal withdrawal analysis regardless of delimiter.

SHA `E82F62CD...` misses paired containers such as `「」`, `『』`, `‹›`, `《》`, `【】`, and `⟦⟧`, allowing an internal middle sentence to look top-level. An intermediate scan prematurely closed on plural/terminal possessives (`students'` / `students’`), so possessive apostrophes must remain lexical while actual quote closers terminate the scope.
